## A Different Question

Knowing how a model works leaves one question open: does it understand what it writes and draws? This course cannot settle that, and serious researchers still disagree. Here are three views, each held for real reasons, so that you have something to reason with. You do not have to choose one today.

**How it works is known far better than whether it understands.**

<!-- STEP id="s2" -->
## View One: It Does Not

Some people answer no, because at every step a model only scores tokens or guesses noise. It has never seen a dragon or tasted ice cream. A false line is learned as smoothly as a true one, and a hand can get six digits, since no step asks what is true. On this view, a model handles patterns and nothing behind them.

**View one: it has patterns, with nothing behind them.**

<!-- STEP id="s3" -->
## View Two: It Does, in Part

Others say that view is too strong. A trained model can handle sentences that were never in its pile, and attention lets one word take its meaning from the words around it. It can also solve new problems by writing out its working, using steps it has seen many times.

A teacher tests a student in just this way, by asking for an idea in a new case. A single brain cell understands nothing either, yet a brain does.

**View two: using an idea well in a new case is a kind of understanding.**

<!-- STEP id="s4" -->
## View Three: It Depends on the Word

A third group says the argument is really about the word "understand". If it means using an idea well, models have some of it. If it means knowing what words point to in the world, or feeling anything, this course showed no sign of that. Evidence is hard to find, because a model's billions of settings have no names and no one can read them all.

**View three: say what you mean by "understand", and keep your reasons ready.**
