## Try the New Sentence

Press Guess on the new sentence. If the model were a list of stored sentences, it would have nothing to find here, because no line in the pile begins with Dadi. Compare its guesses with the sentence above, which did come from the pile.

**A new sentence is one that is not in the pile.**

<!-- STEP id="s2" -->
## Why It Could Guess

Look at the marked words. They come in four lines of the pile, and chai followed them each time, so every nudge on those lines pushed up the same slider. That slider now holds a pattern, not one sentence, and the new sentence uses it too. Here we marked each sentence's kind by hand, but a real model works this out from the words.

**Practice on many similar lines builds a pattern, and a pattern works on new sentences.**

<!-- STEP id="s3" -->
## A Pile Too Small

Turn on the tiny pile, which keeps only 2 lines, then press Guess. The word list is still the same 10 words, but these nudges pushed up only chai and bat, and pushed the rest down. With one chai line, the model cannot tell which words mattered: Amma, drank, or a hot cup. So on Dadi's sentence it has no real reason to prefer chai over bat.

Learning one line exactly like this is called memorising, and it looks perfect on that line but fails on a new one.

**A small pile tends to be memorised, and a large, varied pile gives patterns.**
