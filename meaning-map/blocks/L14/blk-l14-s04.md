## Averaging Real Pictures
You can test the claim about averages with real numbers. Each number in this picture is the average of the same number in all the pictures you have added, worked out live on your device.

Press Add one more, one picture at a time, and watch what happens to the edges.

**Add the pictures one by one and look closely.**

<!-- STEP id="s2" -->
## Why the Edges Go Soft
Every beach has its waterline, clouds and trees in a different place. An edge in one picture falls on plain sand or sky in another, so averaging wipes it out.

What survives is what the pictures share, which for beaches is a lighter top and a warmer bottom. Try the other kinds too.

**An average keeps what pictures share and loses the rest.**

## Which Guess Is Least Wrong?
Now treat each picture as a guess. The meter measures how far that guess is from every picture in the set, and a taller bar means more wrong.

Tap several pictures and note their wrongness, then press Guess the average.

**Try each guess before you decide which is best.**

<!-- STEP id="s4" -->
## Blurred but Least Wrong
The average has the lowest wrongness, though it matches none of the pictures. A sharp guess is exactly right for one picture and badly wrong for the others.

This set is small and a real model trained on far more. At heavy noise it faces the same choice, so its first guess is soft.

**The blur is the least wrong answer when many pictures fit.**
