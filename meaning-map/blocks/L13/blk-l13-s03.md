## Your Turn
Each of the six is a different picture, so one answer gives you no help with the next. Pick what you think each one shows, even if you are only guessing, because the answer appears after you choose.

The number on each picture is its noise level, from 0 to 100. Finish all six before you move on.

**Choose an answer every time, then look.**

<!-- STEP id="s2" -->
## Light Noise Has One Answer
Here the same noise sits on four different pictures, at a light level. They still look different, because the noise moved each number only a little and the shapes survived.

So only one picture could be underneath, which means the noise can be taken away. Taking noise away is called denoising.

**At light noise, one answer fits.**

<!-- STEP id="s3" -->
## Heavy Noise Has Many Answers
Now the same four pictures sit at heavy noise. Drag the slider to find the level where they begin to look alike.

Up here any of the four could be underneath, and so could thousands of other pictures. If you were right about the first pictures, that was mostly luck.

**At heavy noise, many answers fit.**

<!-- STEP id="s4" -->
## Why Sliding Back Is Not Undoing
In the first scene, sliding down brought back the exact beach. That worked only because the computer had kept its random numbers, and a model given a noisy picture does not have them.

So a model is in your position. It can take away a little noise when the answer is clear, but it cannot recover one exact picture from heavy noise.

**Heavy noise cannot be undone exactly.**
