## Three Jobs in a Word Problem

A word problem is a story, so you must choose the steps. Solving one takes three jobs: interpret, calculate and check. Job 1 is to interpret. Step 1: find the quantities.

**Here: 4 packets, 6 pens in each, and 5 pens given away.**

<!-- STEP id="s2" -->
## Step 2: How They Relate

Next, link the quantities. Equal groups mean multiply, and giving away means take away.

Press Step. Our toy counts a made-up pile of worked word problems. It writes the step that came next most often.

**Common links are equal groups, sharing, differences, increases and percentages.**

<!-- STEP id="s3" -->
## "Each" Does Not Always Mean Multiply

Look at the word "each" alone in the pile. Sometimes multiply came next, and sometimes divide. "Each packet contains 6" points to multiply. "The same number in each" points to divide.

**The words and numbers around a word decide the step.**

<!-- STEP id="s4" -->
## "Left" Does Not Always Mean Subtract

"How many are left" points to subtract. But "had 30 left" points to add.

It asks how many there were at first. Our toy counts only nearby words and numbers. A real model uses far more of the story.

**So interpreting is not simple keyword matching.**
