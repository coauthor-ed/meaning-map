## Four Questions, Four Answers

The last lesson ended on a problem: a model always has a next token, even when it has no true fact to use. Here are four questions a student might type, with four answers in the style a chatbot gives. We wrote these by hand, because real products keep changing. Read all four before you judge them.

**Each answer reads like a helpful teacher.**

<!-- STEP id="s2" -->
## Exact Details

Look at the marked details: a year, a book and its author, the dates of a life, and a line of code. You might expect a machine to say "maybe" when it is unsure, as a careful student would. These answers show no doubt at all, and exact details feel like proof, because people usually give them only after checking.

**Exact details can make an answer feel checked.**

<!-- STEP id="s3" -->
## What Support Means

An answer has support when a source outside the model says the same thing, such as a textbook, a library record or a running program. How an answer sounds is not support, so the words on these cards cannot settle the matter.

**Support comes from outside the answer, not from how the answer sounds.**
