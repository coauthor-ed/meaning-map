## Two Things, One Name

People say "the AI" as if it were one thing, but a chatbot is two things working together. The model is the trained settings, and they stay frozen. The app is the ordinary program around them: the chat box, the buttons and any tools. The model's numbers look nothing up, because numbers alone cannot open a web page.

**The model writes text, and the app around it can do other jobs.**

<!-- STEP id="s2" -->
## Search Arrives as Text

Many apps can search the web, and this is how that reaches the model. Press Search and follow the tokens. The app finds a page, copies some of its text and pastes it into the context window beside your question. The model then reads everything in that window and writes, as it always does.

**A search result reaches the model as more text to read, and no setting changes.**

<!-- STEP id="s3" -->
## Choose the Tool

Other questions need other help, and many apps have several tools. Choose a question, then think about what its answer depends on: today's events, exact arithmetic, or neither.

For a big sum, a calculator is surer, and it works as an outside checker. The model can also work a sum out on paper, as the next lesson shows. Now choose what the app should do and read the reply. Try a poor choice as well, because the reply will still sound sure.

**The right tool depends on what the question needs.**

<!-- STEP id="s4" -->
## When the Page Is Wrong

Search does not turn the model into a fact checker. Switch on the wrong page and press Search again. The model can use only the text in its context window, so it has no other way to judge what that page says. For this reason many apps show their sources, and you can open them and decide for yourself.

**Search gives the model more text to read, not a check of the truth.**

<!-- STEP id="s5" -->
## Saying "I Don't Know"

Some chatbots say "I don't know", and some apps refuse a request. That can help, but it is not a truth check. Those words are tokens too, which training can make more likely, and an app can add its own rules. Compare these two answers with their outside checks: doubt in the words and the real result do not always match.

**"I don't know" is text the model can write, not proof that it checked anything.**
