## A Shadow of the Animal Box

Here is the animal box from the first slide, with the goat's "Eats meat?" score at 0. Adding that question added a direction, and now we take one away. Press "Shine the light", and a light shines from the front through the box, so each animal's shadow falls on the back wall. The wall has only two directions, size and tameness, so the shadow cannot keep the third score.

**A shadow keeps some numbers and drops the rest, so two different things can land on one spot.**

## A Flat Shadow of 300 Numbers

The word map does the same thing with much more. A screen has only two directions, so it presses each word's 300 numbers down to two. Each dot is a word's shadow, and 298 of its numbers are lost on the way. Tap a dot to see its 5 nearest words, which are worked out from all 300 numbers.

**The word map is a flat shadow of 300 numbers.**

<!-- STEP id="s3" -->
## The Light Moves

A shadow changes when the light moves, and a word map changes in the same way. On the left, king and batsman almost touch. We can flatten the same 300 numbers a second way, and the panel opens after you choose.

**Choose first: will these two words stay close or move apart?**

<!-- STEP id="s4" -->
## Which One to Trust

The embeddings did not change, only the flattening did. Each flat view drops most of what 300 numbers hold, so two dots can touch by accident. We chose the pairs that change the most, so these are worst cases, not typical ones. The map is good for a first look, and it can mislead you.

**When two dots look near, check the list of nearest words.**
