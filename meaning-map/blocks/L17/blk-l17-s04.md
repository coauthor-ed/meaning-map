## Which One Is the Photo?

Many pictures reach your phone with no label at all, so here is a test. Look closely at these two, as you would look at something a friend sends you, and tap the one that a camera took.

**Make your choice before you read on.**

## Neither

Neither is a photograph. Stable Diffusion 1.5 made both from the prompts now shown, and the man does not exist. If you picked one, your eyes did nothing wrong, because the model learned from a great many real photographs. Faults such as a six-digit hand are getting rarer in newer models, so a list of faults to look for soon goes out of date.

**Looking alone does not settle whether a picture is real.**

<!-- STEP id="s3" -->
## What Does Help

Since looking cannot settle it, ask where the picture came from:

- Who first posted it, and can you find the original?
- Does a source you trust show the same thing?
- Would a lie here help someone?

A real photo can now be doubted too, because a person shown in it can falsely say that it was faked.

**Seeing a picture is no longer proof, so check its source.**

<!-- STEP id="s4" -->
## Deepfakes

A deepfake is a made picture, video or voice showing a real person doing or saying what they never did. Some show school students, and the hurt is real though the picture is false. In India, making or sharing one can break the law. If one shows you or a classmate:

- Tell a parent, a teacher or another adult you trust.
- Do not share it, even to warn a friend.
- Note where you saw it. An adult can report it at cybercrime.gov.in or on helpline 1930.
- If you have no adult to tell, call the Child Helpline on 1098.

**If it happens to you, it is not your fault, and an adult can help.**
