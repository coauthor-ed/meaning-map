## One Set of Settings

You might imagine two parts inside a model, a list of facts and a separate skill with patterns. That picture is wrong, because a model has only one set of settings. A memorised line and a pattern built from many lines both come from those same numbers. Our Toy shows this with the Calcutta line and the chai pattern.

**Memorised lines and patterns live in the same settings.**

<!-- STEP id="s2" -->
## Numbers Do Not Look Things Up

When the model finishes "Calcutta is in", it does not search through stored sentences. Press Next step to follow our Toy. Each word it reads picks its own sliders, the sliders for each answer are added into a score, and each score becomes a chance.

Then search the settings for the line "Calcutta, France". They hold no sentence to open and correct, and the wrong answer comes from one slider that the false line pushed up.

A real model multiplies and adds billions of numbers, in dozens of rounds. Many apps add a search tool around a model, which we come to soon.

**A model's answer is calculated from its settings. It is not found in a list.**

## A Pile That Leans

Now nothing in the pile is false. Finish "My neighbour has a pet" with the Mixed pile, then switch to the Cats-only pile. Every line in both piles could be true, but look at what happens to the other animals. When results lean one way because of what the pile holds, we call that bias, and it can happen with no false line anywhere.

**Bias: when a pile leans one way, the model's answers lean the same way.**
